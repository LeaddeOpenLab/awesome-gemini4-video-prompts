## Prompt (source language)

```text
Build an interactive 3D web demo: **"Night Tokyo — Railroad Crossing"** (踏切).

A small Japanese railroad crossing on a rainy Tokyo night, presented as a floating diorama. It is part of a series of minimal "Night Tokyo" miniatures, so the look matters more than the feature count: one place, rich materials, light and motion you want to keep watching.

## Deliverable
- One self-contained `index.html` in the current folder. Open it and it works: no build step, no server-only features.
- Three.js only, loaded from a CDN via an import map with an exact pinned version. Official Three.js addons (postprocessing, controls, etc.) are fine. No other libraries.
- No external assets: no image textures, models, fonts or audio files. Everything is procedural (geometry, canvas textures, shaders, Web Audio).

## The scene
- A cut-out slab (about 6×6 m) floating in darkness: a narrow wet asphalt street crossing a double track at a right angle. The rails run through the road surface between crossing panels; outside the road, sleepers and gravel ballast continue to the cut edges. The cut edges show a cross-section (asphalt layers, ballast, sleepers). Beyond the slab: darkness and falling rain.
- The crossing equipment, on both sides of the tracks: a signal post with a black-and-yellow striped pole, the X-shaped crossbuck sign, a pair of red lamps facing the road, a direction indicator showing which way a train is coming, a barrier arm with black-and-yellow stripes, and a small equipment box. At most one more prop (e.g. a street lamp).
- Light: at rest the scene is lit by a single street lamp and the faint glow of the equipment. When a train is called, the flashing red lamps take over, and the passing train adds its headlight and lit windows.
- Rain: streaks visible in the light, ripples in the puddles.
- Wet ground: convincing reflections of the lamps in the asphalt and puddles. The alternating red flashes reflected in the wet road are the key shot.
- Palette: deep blue-black night, the street lamp's light, saturated red from the crossing lamps, the train's interior light. Subtle bloom is fine; don't wash everything out.
- Small signs of life: e.g. the street lamp occasionally flickers, the rain intensity varies slightly. Rare, small, believable.

## Interaction
- Click the scene (a click, not a drag) or press `T` to call a train. The crossing comes alive: the bell starts ringing, the red lamps flash alternately, the direction indicator shows where the train is coming from, and the barrier arms lower smoothly.
- Within about 5 seconds a short commuter train (2–3 cars, fictional livery) comes out of the darkness at one cut edge, rushes through the crossing and disappears into the darkness at the other: headlight, lit windows whose light sweeps across the wet ground, spray from the rails. Once it has passed, the bell stops, the lamps go dark and the barriers rise.
- Trains alternate direction and use the track for their direction. Calling again while the crossing is active sends a second train the other way: the indicator shows both directions and the barriers stay down until the last train has passed.
- Sound, synthesized with Web Audio: rain ambience, the crossing bell, the approaching rumble, wheels clacking over rail joints, the pass-by. Audio starts after the first user interaction. `M` toggles mute.
- Camera: a strong default 3/4 shot at roughly eye level, with the nearest signal post and its barrier in the foreground and the tracks running across the frame, and a slow, subtle idle drift. Drag to orbit within limited angles, scroll to zoom within limits. `C` resets to the default shot.
- UI: none, except a tiny unobtrusive hint that fades out after a few seconds.

## Quality bar
- Must look good at any aspect ratio, including narrow ones (around 2:3 and 9:16): it will be recorded side by side with two other versions.
- Smooth on a mid-range laptop: target 60 fps, cap devicePixelRatio at 2.
- No console errors.

## How to work
Work autonomously and don't ask clarifying questions; where something is unspecified, make the choice you think looks best. If your environment lets you open the page and look at it, do so and iterate on the visuals until you are happy with them. When finished, stop and briefly list what you built.
```
