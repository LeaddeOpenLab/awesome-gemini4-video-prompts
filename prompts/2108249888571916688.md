# Propane Tank Crush and Explosion WebGPU Simulation

## Prompt (English)

```text
EXECUTION RULES (read first)
- Build in this order:
  1. a minimal render skeleton;
  2. compile every WGSL shader (render and compute) and create every pipeline on the real WebGPU device;
  3. only then build the rest on top.
  Never write large amounts of shader code without compiling it.
- Checkpoints: after each major milestone, save the current working build as the single HTML file and present it, so an interruption never loses work.
- Every automated check must finish in under 60 seconds. Use sampling instead of dense sweeps; if a check would take longer, reduce the samples and report the coverage.
- Budget: at most 300k rendered triangles. Keep the simulation sized so it runs in real time on a laptop and a recent phone. Report the final counts.
- Keep screenshots rare: software GPU rendering is slow. Render small (e.g. 800x500) unless checking fine detail.
- Keep progress messages short.

Build "Don't Press It." — a single self-contained HTML file (embedded JS and CSS) running a genuine WebGPU + WGSL interactive physics experiment. A hydraulic press crushes a small 5-litre steel propane cylinder. The viewer picks the press force and whether there is an ignition source, then hits a big red "do not press" button. Everything that happens — denting, buckling, pressure rise, rupture, gas release, frost, fire — must come from live simulation, not animation. No 3D engine or physics libraries; all geometry, materials and physics are written in code and computed live. Google Fonts are allowed, with system fallbacks. Show a clear message if WebGPU is unavailable.

THE LOOK (bold, eye-catching)
- A dark graphite industrial studio: polished concrete floor, strong overhead spotlight plus a cool rim light, so the orange and yellow pop.
- The press: a heavy four-column hydraulic press with a dark steel frame, a chrome hydraulic ram and a big flat steel platen.
  - The platen's sides and the lower bed edges carry bold black-yellow diagonal hazard stripes.
  - A large analog force gauge on the frame, with a needle in tonnes.
- The cylinder:
  - glossy safety-orange paint with huge white stencil letters "GAS" on its side;
  - a brass valve on top and a protective steel collar around it;
  - a steel foot ring at the bottom.
  - About 200 mm diameter and 300 mm tall body, 2 mm steel wall, welded at the middle seam. It lies on its side on the press bed.
  - Paint flakes and cracks where the steel bends hard.

THE PHYSICS
- Steel:
  - an elastoplastic thin-shell simulation (finite elements or a dense mass-spring shell with plasticity);
  - yield around 300 MPa, with work hardening;
  - realistic buckling and folding as the platen comes down;
  - the end domes, collar and foot ring are much stiffer than the body;
  - no pre-baked fold patterns.
- Contents:
  - about 2 kg of liquefied propane, filling about 80% of the volume as liquid, with vapour above;
  - internal pressure follows propane's vapour pressure (about 8 bar at 20 °C);
  - as the platen flattens the body, the vapour space shrinks and vapour condenses, so pressure stays near the vapour pressure;
  - once the vapour space is gone, the liquid is nearly incompressible and pressure rises sharply.
- Rupture:
  - the shell fails where stress or strain actually exceeds the limit: the weld seam, a sharp fold, or the valve shearing off;
  - the failure point must come from the simulation, not be chosen in advance.
- Force:
  - the press pushes with the chosen maximum force and stops descending when the cylinder's resistance equals it;
  - resistance = steel stiffness + internal pressure × contact area;
  - with the chosen forces, the expected behaviour is:
    - 1 t: barely dents, holds;
    - 10 t: heavy flattening, may hold;
    - 100 t: ruptures.
  - Results must emerge from the physics; if your simulation disagrees, report it honestly rather than forcing it.

THE RELEASE
- On rupture, a loud bang and a two-phase jet of propane bursts from the failure point.
- The jet's reaction force can jerk or spin the cylinder.
- The released liquid flash-boils:
  - a dense white cloud (propane fog plus condensed air moisture) billows out;
  - the shell around the opening quickly frosts over as its temperature drops toward −42 °C;
  - frost spreads realistically.
- Propane is heavier than air: the cloud must sink and spread along the floor rather than rise. Simulate it as a GPU smoke or density field with buoyancy (negative here) and advection.
- Spark OFF: no fire. Just the bang, the jet, the sinking cloud and the frost.
- Spark ON:
  - a small spark igniter sits on the floor about 1 m from the press, clicking every second;
  - fire starts only when gas with a flammable concentration (about 2–10% propane in air) actually reaches it;
  - the flame front then spreads through the flammable part of the cloud as a fireball, its size tied to how much gas has mixed into the flammable range;
  - flames lick back toward the jet, then burn out as the gas is consumed.
- Slow motion (0.2x) eases in at the moment of rupture for about 3 real seconds. It slows the simulation itself, never playback.

CAMERA
- Free orbit:
  - drag to rotate;
  - mouse wheel or pinch to zoom in and out;
  - right-drag or two-finger drag to pan.
- Smooth damping, sensible limits: no going under the floor, no going inside the press.
- Default view: a dramatic low three-quarter angle.
- The viewer can orbit freely at any time, including during the crush and the fire.
- A "View" button resets the camera.

UI
- Top-left:
  - small caps label "UNDER PRESSURE — NO. 05";
  - title "Don't Press It." in a large bold italic serif;
  - one short subtitle line.
- Live readouts, compact, top-right:
  - press force (t);
  - ram travel (mm);
  - internal pressure (bar);
  - shell temperature (°C);
  - liquid fill (%).
- Bottom-centre, the control deck:
  - a segmented selector for press force: 1 t / 10 t / 100 t;
  - a Spark toggle: Off / On;
  - in the middle, a big glossy red mushroom button inside a black-yellow hazard ring, labelled "DO NOT PRESS", under a clear flip-up safety cover. First click flips the cover open; second click presses the button and the press starts descending.
  - The button lights up while the press runs and greys out until Reset.
- Reset (R).
- Mouse and touch. Nothing may overlap at 390x844, 844x390 or 1440x900.

SOUND (Web Audio, synthesized, mute toggle)
- Hydraulic hum rising with force.
- Metal creaks and groans while buckling.
- A sharp bang at rupture.
- The roar and hiss of the jet.
- Spark clicks.
- A deep whoomp for ignition.
- Volumes tied to real values.

VALIDATION
- Run all six combinations headless (3 forces × spark on/off) and report for each:
  - final ram travel;
  - peak internal pressure;
  - whether it ruptured, and where;
  - whether it ignited.
- Confirm that with Spark OFF nothing ever ignites.
- Confirm that the gas cloud sinks.
- Check that the total propane mass (inside + released) is conserved within 2%.
- No NaN or exploding elements.
- Report frame time during the release.

AT THE END
Write a short, honest report:
- what is truly simulated;
- what is approximated or faked (including smoke, frost and fire);
- how the failure point and ignition are decided;
- the six-combination results;
- final triangle, element and particle counts.

COST REPORT (last item of your final reply)
- Report the token usage for this whole task, broken down as:
  - regular input tokens;
  - cached input tokens (cache reads);
  - cache write tokens;
  - output tokens (including reasoning).
- Use the exact session usage counter if your environment exposes one. If it does not, estimate, and clearly mark every number as "estimated".
- Convert it to US dollars at the current public API pay-as-you-go price for the exact model you are running on. State the per-million-token prices you used for each category.
- Also report the total working time from the first message to the final build.
- Format:
  Input: X tokens · $X
  Cached reads: X tokens · $X
  Cache writes: X tokens · $X
  Output: X tokens · $X
  Total: $X · working time X h X min
```
